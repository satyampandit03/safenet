📚 Safety guides

Simple, clear advice for students on staying safe online.

🚨 What is cyberbullying?

▼
Cyberbullying is when someone uses the internet, phones, or apps to hurt, threaten, embarrass, or exclude another person repeatedly. It can happen on Instagram, WhatsApp, YouTube comments, online games, and more. Signs include: getting mean messages, being left out of group chats deliberately, someone spreading rumors about you online, or your photos being shared without permission.

📸 Take screenshots as evidence

▼
Before blocking or deleting anything, take a screenshot of the messages, posts, or comments. This is your proof. Save the date and time if visible. On Android: press Volume Down + Power. On iPhone: press Side button + Volume Up. Store these in a safe folder. Evidence is important if you want to report to your school, parents, or police.

🚫 How to block and report

▼
On Instagram: tap the profile → three dots → Block/Report. On WhatsApp: tap the name → three dots → Block. On YouTube: click the three dots on any comment → Report. On Snapchat: tap the name → press and hold → Block. Always report AND block — reporting helps the platform take action on the bully's account.

🔒 Check your privacy settings

▼
Set your Instagram and Snapchat to Private so only approved followers see your content. On WhatsApp, go to Settings → Privacy → set Last Seen, Profile Photo, and Status to "My Contacts" only. Never share your location publicly. Avoid accepting friend requests from strangers. Use a strong password and enable two-factor authentication.

💬 Tell a trusted adult

▼
You don't have to handle this alone. Tell a parent, teacher, school counselor, or any adult you trust. It can feel scary or embarrassing, but adults can help in ways you can't on your own — like talking to the bully's parents, contacting the school, or filing a complaint. If you're afraid to talk in person, write it in a note or send a text.

🧠 Protect your mental health

▼
Being bullied online can feel overwhelming and deeply hurtful. Remember: it is NOT your fault. Take breaks from social media. Spend time with people who care about you. Write in a journal. If you feel very sad, anxious, or don't want to go to school, please talk to a counselor. Calling iCall (9152987821) or Childline (1098) is always an option.
